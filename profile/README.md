## Why we exist

A scanner is paid by the finding, so it can never afford to dismiss one.

That is how a team ends up with thousands of alerts, a severity column nobody
trusts, and no answer to the only question that actually matters: **which of
these can be reached, and what happens if it is?**

Arvion is built the other way round. Instead of software you operate, you get
engineers you employ — machines that read the code, prove a vulnerability by
walking the attack end to end, then fix it where it lives.

They exploit it. They fix it. **You say when.**

## The Three Laws

> **First Law.** An engineer may not claim more than it proved — nor, by staying
> silent, let you believe you are safe when you are not.
>
> **Second Law.** An engineer works to the clearance you set and no further —
> except where obeying would break the First Law — and hands you every call
> that is yours.
>
> **Third Law.** An engineer guards the one thing that lets it exist — your
> trust — but never by hiding a failure, faking a preview, booking a win it did
> not earn, or reaching past the clearance you set.
>
> — the Laws, [arvion.ai/manifesto](https://arvion.ai/manifesto)

Honesty is the feature, not the disclaimer. Every finding lands in one of three
places, and we say which:

- **Acted on** — reachable, proven, fix written and PR'd.
- **Ruled out** — a real vulnerability on a path nothing can reach, filed with the reasoning.
- **Assumed** — we could not prove it either way, and we tell you that instead of picking a comfortable answer.
